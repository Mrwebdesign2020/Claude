# 10K in 8 weeks: Runna's approach, worked out and applied

**Runner:** 34 years old, 93 kg, used to play amateur football and had a big engine back then.
**Goal:** run 10 km without stopping by **Sunday 24 January 2027**.
**Plan dates:** Monday 30 Nov 2026 to Sunday 24 Jan 2027 (8 weeks).

---

## Part 1: How Runna builds a plan

This comes from how the app behaves in public: its onboarding, its plans and its workout screens. It is not from Runna's code.

### 1. Onboarding asks for five things
| Input | What Runna uses it for |
|---|---|
| Goal (distance + date) | Sets how many weeks the plan has and the target race |
| Current ability (a recent 5K/10K time, or "beginner") | Sets your **pace zones**, the core of every workout |
| Runs per week + which days | How many sessions, and where the hard and easy days fall |
| Long-run day | Puts the long run on the weekend, with an easy day before it |
| Experience level | How fast weekly volume grows and how hard the intervals are |

### 2. All paces come from one fitness number
Runna turns your benchmark time into a predicted race time with a VDOT- or Riegel-style formula. Every session pace comes from that prediction:
- **Easy / long run:** 10K pace + 60–90 s/km (most of your running)
- **Tempo:** about 10K pace − 5–10 s/km ("comfortably hard")
- **Intervals:** 5K pace or a bit faster
- **Strides:** short and fast, focused on form, not timed

### 3. Each week has three types of run
1. **Easy run:** builds the aerobic base. Holding back is the point.
2. **Quality session:** intervals or tempo. Raises the speed you can hold.
3. **Long run:** builds endurance and grows by about 1 km a week.

Roughly 75–80% of your weekly time is easy. 20–25% is hard.

### 4. The plan moves through phases
**Base → Build → Peak → Taper**, with a lighter **deload week every 3–4 weeks**. Weekly volume rises about 10–20% a week, from a low start.

### 5. Paces update as you go
After key sessions and check-in runs, Runna updates the predicted time and moves all your paces up or down. If you miss sessions, it shifts the week around rather than stacking them up.

### 6. Strength and mobility come as extras
Short strength sessions protect the tendons and joints that take the impact.

## Part 2: What this means for you

- **Your heart and lungs will come back faster than your tendons.** Football fitness is real, but 93 kg on the road is roughly 2.5× bodyweight on the calves and Achilles with every stride. That is why the plan starts with run-walk, even if it feels too easy. Most ex-athletes who get injured got hurt by doing too much too soon, not by being unfit.
- **Football gave you repeated sprints, not steady running.** You'll probably like the intervals and find the easy runs hard to keep slow. Keep them slow anyway.
- **3 runs + 2 short strength sessions a week.** Enough to reach 10K without overloading your legs.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Strength 20' | **Easy run** | Rest / walk / bike | **Quality** | Strength 20' | Rest | **Long run** |

## Part 3: Your pace zones

**Week 1 Thursday benchmark:** run 2 km as hard as you can **hold evenly**. Then:
**Estimated 5K time = your 2 km time × 2.64**

Find the row closest to your estimated 5K time:

| Estimated 5K | 5K / interval pace | **10K goal pace** (predicted 10K) | Tempo pace | **Easy pace** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 27:00 | 5:24 /km | 5:38 /km (≈56:20) | ~5:30 | 6:40–7:10 |
| 30:00 | 6:00 /km | 6:15 /km (≈62:30) | ~6:05 | 7:15–7:45 |
| 33:00 | 6:36 /km | 6:53 /km (≈68:50) | ~6:45 | 7:55–8:25 |
| 36:00 | 7:12 /km | 7:30 /km (≈75:00) | ~7:20 | 8:30–9:00 or run-walk |

**Heart-rate backup** (estimated max HR about 185): keep easy runs **under ~145 bpm**. If it goes over, slow down or walk for 30 s. The **talk test** is also fine: on easy runs you should be able to speak in full sentences.

**Week 4 check-in:** you'll run a 5K (a parkrun works well). Use that time to pick a new row in the table. This is what Runna does when it updates your paces.

## Part 4: The 8-week plan

WU = warm-up (10 min easy jog plus a few leg swings). CD = cool-down (5–10 min easy jog or walk).

### BASE

**Week 1 (30 Nov–6 Dec): wake the legs up** · ~9 km
- **Tue:** 5 min brisk walk → 6 × (3 min easy jog / 1 min walk)
- **Thu:** WU → **2 km benchmark** (even, hard effort, write down the time) → CD
- **Sun:** 30 min as 4 min jog / 1 min walk, very easy

**Week 2 (7–13 Dec)** · ~12 km
- **Tue:** 25 min easy, nonstop if HR allows
- **Thu:** WU → 6 × (1 min at 5K pace / 2 min walk-jog) → CD
- **Sun:** 35 min easy (~5 km), walk breaks allowed

**Week 3 (14–20 Dec)** · ~14 km
- **Tue:** 30 min easy
- **Thu:** WU → 5 × (3 min at 10K pace / 90 s jog) → CD
- **Sun:** **6 km** easy, nonstop

**Week 4 (21–27 Dec): DELOAD + CHECK-IN** · ~12 km
- **Tue:** 25 min easy + 4 × 20 s strides (walk back between them)
- **Thu:** WU → 2 × 8 min tempo / 2 min jog → CD
- **Sun:** **5K check-in** (parkrun or solo), comfortably hard → update your pace row

### BUILD

**Week 5 (28 Dec–3 Jan)** · ~16 km
- **Tue:** 35 min easy + 4 strides
- **Thu:** WU → 4 × (5 min at 10K pace / 2 min jog) → CD
- **Sun:** **7.5 km** easy

**Week 6 (4–10 Jan)** · ~18 km
- **Tue:** 35 min easy
- **Thu:** WU → **20 min continuous tempo** → CD
- **Sun:** **8.5 km** easy

### PEAK

**Week 7 (11–17 Jan): hardest week** · ~21 km
- **Tue:** 40 min easy + 6 strides
- **Thu:** WU → 3 × (10 min at **10K goal pace** / 2 min jog) → CD. You'll have run 30 min at goal pace.
- **Sun:** **9.5 km** easy. That's enough; you don't need to run 10K before race day.

### TAPER

**Week 8 (18–24 Jan): get fresh** · ~14 km + race
- **Tue:** 30 min easy + 4 strides
- **Thu:** WU → 3 × 3 min at goal pace / 2 min jog → CD
- **Sat:** rest, or a 15 min shakeout jog
- **Sun 24 Jan: 10K.** Run the first 3 km 5–10 s/km *slower* than goal pace, settle in at km 3–7, and push only from km 8.

## Part 5: Strength (Mon + Fri, about 20 min)

This matters a lot at 93 kg.

| Exercise | Sets × reps | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Single-leg calf raise (straight knee) | 3 × 12–15 each | Achilles / calf: the #1 injury spot for heavier runners |
| Bent-knee calf raise | 3 × 12–15 each | Soleus, which takes most of the load when you run |
| Bulgarian split squat | 3 × 8 each | Quads, glutes, knee stability |
| Single-leg Romanian deadlift | 3 × 8 each | Hamstrings, balance |
| Glute bridge (single-leg when easy) | 3 × 12 | Hips: stops the knee caving inward |
| Plank + side plank | 3 × 30–45 s | Keeps your form steady late in a run |

## Part 6: Rules for adjusting (Runna's logic, done by hand)

- **Missed one run:** skip it. Don't make it up. If it was the long run, swap it with Tuesday.
- **Missed a whole week:** repeat the previous week.
- **Pain that changes how you run, or gets worse during a run:** stop and take 2–3 days off. Bike or swim instead.
- **Normal soreness (DOMS), under 3/10, that eases as you warm up:** fine.
- **Easy runs feel too easy?** Good. Don't speed them up. Run quality sessions faster only if the 5K check-in says you can.
- **Quality session feels awful?** Turn it into an easy run. One bad session doesn't matter.
- **Shoes:** get proper cushioned trainers fitted at a running shop. Indoor football shoes or old sneakers won't do.
- **Before starting:** if you have any heart or blood-pressure history or chest symptoms, see your GP first.
