The groin rule

Score the groin from 0 to 10 after every run. This rule overrides the schedule.

0–3 /10Back to normal the next morning? Carry on with the plan.
4–5 /10Or still sore the next morning: repeat this week, don't move up.
Above 5Getting worse during the run, or limping: stop, 3 days without running (cycling is fine if pain-free). See a physio if it doesn't settle.

Protect the groin

  • Run on flat, even ground. No hills or sloping road edges for the first 4 weeks.
  • Short, quick steps. Long strides strain the groin.
  • Speed comes in slowly: easy only in weeks 1–2, moderate efforts from week 3, relaxed strides from week 4 if the groin scores 0–2. Never all-out sprints.
  • Don't massage or foam-roll hard on the calcified spot.
  • If it hurts in daily life or no physio has seen it recently, get it checked before starting. Calcification there can be different conditions, and the safe load depends on which one.

How Runna builds a plan, and how this one copies it

Worked out from how the app behaves in public, not from Runna's code.

  1. OnboardingGoal and date, current ability, days per week, long-run day. Here: 10K on 24 Jan, 10 min nonstop, 3 runs, Sunday long run.
  2. One fitness numberA benchmark time is turned into a predicted race time; every pace comes from it. Here: the week-4 3 km check-in.
  3. Three runs a weekEasy run, quality session (intervals or tempo), long run. About 80% of the running is easy.
  4. PhasesBase, Build, Peak, Taper, with a lighter week every 3–4 weeks. Here: week 4 is the deload.
  5. Paces updateCheck-ins recalculate paces; missed sessions are moved, not doubled up.
  6. Strength as an extraShort sessions protect tendons and joints. Here: groin and calf work, because of your injury and your 93 kg.

Missed one run? Skip it; if it was the long run, swap it with Tuesday. Missed a week? Repeat the previous one. Shoes: cushioned trainers fitted at a running shop. Heart or blood-pressure history? See your GP first.
