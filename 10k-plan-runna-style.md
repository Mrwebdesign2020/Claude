# 10K in 8 weeks: Runna's approach, worked out and applied

**Runner:** 34 years old, 93 kg, used to play amateur football and had a big engine back then.
**Starting point:** can jog **10 min without stopping**. History of a **groin injury with calcification between the hip and the thigh** (*calcificatie tussen heup en bovenbeen*).
**Goal:** run 10 km without stopping by **Sunday 24 January 2027**. Back-up dates if the groin needs more time: 31 Jan or 7 Feb.
**Plan dates:** Monday 30 Nov 2026 to Sunday 24 Jan 2027 (8 weeks).

> **Before you start:** this plan assumes the groin doesn't hurt in daily life and stays quiet when you jog for 10 minutes. If it's still painful, or no physio or sports doctor has looked at it recently, get it checked first. Calcification near the groin can be several different things (for example myositis ossificans or calcific tendinopathy), and the right load depends on which one it is. Show them this plan.

## Part 1: How Runna builds a plan

This comes from how the app behaves in public: its onboarding, its plans and its workout screens. It is not from Runna's code.

### 1. Onboarding asks for five things
| Input | What Runna uses it for |
|---|---|
| Goal (distance + date) | Sets how many weeks the plan has and the target race |
| Current ability (a recent 5K/10K time, or "beginner") | Sets your **pace zones**, the core of every workout |
| Runs per week + which days | How many sessions, and where the hard and easy days fall |
| Long-run day | Puts the long run on the weekend, with an easy day before it |
| Experience level | How fast weekly volume grows and how hard the intervals are |

### 2. All paces come from one fitness number
Runna turns your benchmark time into a predicted race time with a VDOT- or Riegel-style formula. Every session pace comes from that prediction:
- **Easy / long run:** 10K pace + 60–90 s/km (most of your running)
- **Tempo:** about 10K pace − 5–10 s/km ("comfortably hard")
- **Intervals:** 5K pace or a bit faster
- **Strides:** short and fast, focused on form, not timed

### 3. Each week has three types of run
1. **Easy run:** builds the aerobic base. Holding back is the point.
2. **Quality session:** intervals or tempo. Raises the speed you can hold.
3. **Long run:** builds endurance and grows by about 1 km a week.

Roughly 75–80% of your weekly time is easy. 20–25% is hard.

### 4. The plan moves through phases
**Base → Build → Peak → Taper**, with a lighter **deload week every 3–4 weeks**. Weekly volume rises about 10–20% a week, from a low start.

### 5. Paces update as you go
After key sessions and check-in runs, Runna updates the predicted time and moves all your paces up or down. If you miss sessions, it shifts the week around rather than stacking them up.

### 6. Strength and mobility come as extras
Short strength sessions protect the tendons and joints that take the impact.

## Part 2: What this means for you

- **The groin decides how fast you build up, not your fitness.** Your heart and lungs remember football. The adductors, hip flexors, calves and Achilles need to be built up again. So the plan uses **run-walk for the first 2 weeks** and adds speed only gradually.
- **Speed is what loads the groin most.** Sprinting, strides, hills and long strides put the most strain on the adductors and hip flexors. So:
  - **Weeks 1–2:** easy running only.
  - **Week 3 onwards:** moderate efforts ("comfortably hard").
  - **Week 4 onwards:** short, relaxed strides, and only if the groin is quiet.
  - **Never all-out sprints.**
- **Getting from 10 minutes to 10K in 8 weeks is doable but tight.** The plan has a built-in fallback: if you're not ready, you run the 10K with short planned walk breaks, or you push the date back 1–2 weeks. Either way you finish it.
- **At 93 kg the calves and Achilles also take a lot of load.** That's why both strength sessions start with them.

## Part 3: The groin rule (check this after every run)

Score the groin from **0 to 10**:

| Signal | What to do |
|---|---|
| During the run **0–3/10**, and back to normal **the next morning** | ✅ Carry on with the plan |
| **4–5/10**, or still sore the next morning | ⚠️ Repeat this week; don't move up |
| **Above 5/10**, getting worse during the run, or you start limping | ⛔ Stop. 3 days without running (cycling is fine if it's pain-free). See a physio if it doesn't settle |

Also:
- Run on **flat, even ground**: path, track or park. No hills or sloping road edges for the first 4 weeks.
- Take **short, quick steps**. Long strides strain the groin.
- **Don't massage or foam-roll hard on the calcified spot.**

## Part 4: Your pace zones

**Weeks 1–3:** don't use pace. Run on feel: you should be able to **talk in full sentences**, or keep your heart rate **under ~145 bpm**.

**Week 4 Sunday check-in:** run 3 km at a **comfortably hard** effort. That means hard but in control; *not* all-out. Then:
**Estimated 5K time = your 3 km time × 1.72**

Find the row closest to your estimated 5K time:

| Estimated 5K | 5K pace | **10K goal pace** (predicted 10K) | Tempo pace | **Easy pace** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 27:00 | 5:24 /km | 5:38 /km (≈56:20) | ~5:30 | 6:40–7:10 |
| 30:00 | 6:00 /km | 6:15 /km (≈62:30) | ~6:05 | 7:15–7:45 |
| 33:00 | 6:36 /km | 6:53 /km (≈68:50) | ~6:45 | 7:55–8:25 |
| 36:00 | 7:12 /km | 7:30 /km (≈75:00) | ~7:20 | 8:30–9:00 |

Because the check-in isn't all-out, these paces come out a bit cautious. That's on purpose.

## Part 5: The 8-week plan

- **WU** = warm-up: 5 min brisk walk, then 5 min easy jog, then gentle leg swings (front–back and side to side).
- **CD** = cool-down: 5–10 min walk.
- **Format "3'/1'"** = 3 min jog, then 1 min walk.

### BASE: from 10 minutes to 25 minutes nonstop

**Week 1 (30 Nov–6 Dec)** · ~5–6 km
- **Tue:** 5' walk → 6 × (2' jog / 1' walk)
- **Thu:** 5' walk → 5 × (3' jog / 1' walk)
- **Sun:** 5' walk → 4 × (5' jog / 1' walk)

**Week 2 (7–13 Dec)** · ~8 km
- **Tue:** 5' walk → 4 × (5' jog / 1' walk)
- **Thu:** 5' walk → 3 × (8' jog / 1' walk)
- **Sun:** 5' walk → 3 × (10' jog / 1' walk) (~33 min)

**Week 3 (14–20 Dec)** · ~11 km
- **Tue:** **25' nonstop** easy
- **Thu:** WU → 4 × (2' comfortably hard / 2' easy jog) → CD. This is your first quality session; keep it in control.
- **Sun:** 2 × (15' jog / 1' walk) (~4.5 km)

**Week 4 (21–27 Dec): DELOAD + CHECK-IN** · ~10 km
- **Tue:** 25' easy
- **Thu:** 20' easy + 4 × 15 s relaxed strides. **Only if the groin is at 0–2/10.** Otherwise skip the strides.
- **Sun:** WU → **3 km check-in** (comfortably hard) → CD. Use the result to pick your pace row.

### BUILD

**Week 5 (28 Dec–3 Jan)** · ~14 km
- **Tue:** 30' easy
- **Thu:** WU → 4 × (4' at 10K pace / 2' jog) → CD
- **Sun:** **40' nonstop** (~5.5–6 km)

**Week 6 (4–10 Jan)** · ~16 km
- **Tue:** 35' easy + 4 relaxed strides
- **Thu:** WU → 2 × (10' tempo / 2' jog) → CD
- **Sun:** **50' nonstop** (~7–7.5 km)

### PEAK

**Week 7 (11–17 Jan): the key week** · ~19 km
- **Tue:** 35' easy + 4 strides
- **Thu:** WU → 3 × (8' at 10K goal pace / 2' jog) → CD
- **Sun:** **60–65' nonstop** (~8.5–9 km). This is your **go/no-go**. Only change the 10K plan if this run *didn't* go well **or** the groin scored 4+:
  - **Plan B:** run the 10K on 24 Jan with **30 s walk breaks every 2 km**. You still complete the full 10K.
  - **Plan C:** repeat week 7 and move the 10K to 31 Jan.

### TAPER

**Week 8 (18–24 Jan)** · ~10 km + race
- **Tue:** 30' easy
- **Thu:** WU → 3 × (3' at goal pace / 2' jog) → CD
- **Sat:** rest
- **Sun 24 Jan: 10K.** Run the first 3 km **slower** than goal pace, settle in for km 3–7, and only push from km 8 if everything feels good.

## Part 6: Strength (Mon + Fri, about 20 min, groin-focused)

| Exercise | Weeks | Sets × reps | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adductor squeeze** (ball or pillow between the knees, lying on your back with knees bent) | 1–8 | 5 × 30 s at ~50–70% effort | Isometric (holding, not moving) work calms the adductor tendon and builds it up |
| **Side-lying hip adduction** (bottom leg lifts) | 1–8 | 3 × 12 each | Adductor strength through movement |
| **Copenhagen plank, short lever** (knee on a bench, top leg supported) | **3–8**, only if 0–2/10 | 3 × 15–20 s each | The best-evidenced exercise against groin injuries; build up slowly |
| Single-leg calf raise (straight knee) | 1–8 | 3 × 12–15 each | Achilles / calf: carries most of your 93 kg |
| Bent-knee calf raise | 1–8 | 3 × 12–15 each | Soleus |
| Glute bridge, then single-leg | 1–8 | 3 × 12 | Hip stability |
| Side plank | 1–8 | 3 × 30 s each | Glute med / hip control |
| Split squat (short range, no deep lunge) | **3–8** | 3 × 8 each | Leg strength without overstretching the groin |

Leave out for now: deep lateral lunges, explosive jumps and heavy stretching of the groin.

## Part 7: Rules for adjusting (Runna's logic, done by hand)

- **Missed one run:** skip it. Don't make it up. If it was the long run, swap it with Tuesday.
- **Missed a whole week:** repeat the previous week. The 10K moves back a week; that's fine.
- **Groin above 3/10:** follow the groin rule in Part 3. It overrides the schedule.
- **Easy runs feel too easy?** Good. Your fitness will come back faster than your tissues.
- **Shoes:** get proper cushioned trainers fitted at a running shop, not indoor football shoes.
- **Heart or blood-pressure history, or chest symptoms:** see your GP first.
